### 1. `ddlj-title` — DDLJ का title Aditya Chopra ने नहीं दिया था?

- **Facts:**
  - The title comes from the song "Le Jayenge Le Jayenge" in *Chor Machaye Shor* (1974); the film carries a "Title suggested by" credit. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dilwale_Dulhania_Le_Jayenge)
  - It passed *Sholay* in 2001 as the longest-running film in Indian cinema and reached 1,000 weeks at Maratha Mandir in December 2014. (same page)
  - In February 2015 Maratha Mandir announced the run would end at 1,009 weeks; it was reinstated after fans and the producers stepped in. (same page)
  - By 16 March 2020 it had run 1,251 weeks, until the COVID lockdown shut the theatre. (same page)
- **Payoff:** Kirron Kher suggested the title.
- **Images:** Posters and publicity stills: credited commentary.
- **Lock before use:** "First film with a 'title suggested by' credit" is the page's "believed" wording, so don't say "first". Cross-check "longest-running" with a trade source. Find an interview for the Kirron Kher credit.

### 2. `mr-india` — Mr. India पहले Amitabh को offer हुई थी?

- **Facts:**
  - Salim–Javed wrote Arun Verma for Rajesh Khanna, then offered it to Amitabh Bachchan, who said no. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mr._India_(1987_film))
  - Mogambo was first offered to Anupam Kher; after the screen test the team felt he looked "more funny than ferocious", and Amrish Puri was cast (per Satish Kaushik, as the page reports). (same page)
  - Anil Kapoor got the lead. (same page)
- **Payoff:** Amitabh's reason, as reported: the real hero is the invisible character, so why does the film need him?
- **Images:** Posters and publicity stills: credited commentary.
- **Lock before use:** The Amitabh quote, and the claim that it led to the Salim–Javed split, rest on one article. Find the original interview, or paraphrase with "reportedly". There is no "watch" in the source (it's a cloaking device). The release date varies (25 vs 29 May 1987), so use the year only.

### 3. `pather-panchali` — Pather Panchali के लिए किसके गहने गिरवी रखे गए?

- **Facts:**
  - Budget about ₹70,000; no producer would back a film with "no stars, songs and action scenes". [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pather_Panchali)
  - Ray pawned his life-insurance policy and sold his gramophone records. (same page)
  - The West Bengal government loan was booked as "roads improvement": officials took the film for a rural-uplift documentary. (same page)
  - Shooting began 27 October 1952, stopped for nearly a year when the money ran out, and took three years in all. (same page)
- **Payoff:** Bijoya Ray, his wife, pawned her jewellery.
- **Images:** Find the infobox poster's file page on Commons and check its licence.
- **Lock before use:** The "$60,000 sale / $700,000 profit" line is unsourced on the page. The page doesn't call it Ray's debut, so add a source before saying "पहली film". Kishore Kumar's ₹5,000 has no primary source.

### 4. `mayabazar` — 1957 की film में चाँदनी असल में camera trick थी

- **Facts:**
  - Shot in Telugu and Tamil at the same time: Telugu out 27 March 1957, Tamil 12 April 1957. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayabazar)
  - The page credits the song "Lahiri Lahiri" with "the first illusion of moonlight in Indian cinema", by cinematographer Marcus Bartley. (same page)
  - A colourised version went out in 45 Andhra Pradesh theatres in January 2010; in 2015 the Film Heritage Foundation opposed digital colourisation while announcing a restoration. (same page)
  - In 2013 CNN-IBN put it on its 100 greatest Indian films, and an online public poll voted it number one among those 100. (same page)
- **Payoff:** The "moonlight" in "Lahiri Lahiri" was a camera trick.
- **Images:** Check Commons (`Mayabazar (1957 film)`); otherwise use credited publicity stills.
- **Lock before use:** Attribute "first illusion of moonlight" ("कहा जाता है") or find a film-history source. Call the poll a poll, not a ranking.

### 5. `pakeezah` — Pakeezah बनने में 15 साल क्यों लगे?

- **Facts:**
  - Principal photography began 16 July 1956; filming was completed in November 1971. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakeezah)
  - Released 4 February 1972, it "initially opened to mediocre box-office returns". (same page)
  - It then became one of the year's highest-grossing Hindi films and ran for over 50 weeks. (same page)
- **Payoff:** Meena Kumari died about a month after release. Trade analysts said the turnaround "might have been due to" her death.
- **Images:** Credited publicity stills or poster. Commons category not found.
- **Lock before use:** This is a sensitive subject, so set `honorific = true`, attribute the analysts' view, and don't mention her addiction. Read the production section for the reason it took 15 years before writing the payoff.

### 6. `kismet` — एक theatre, एक film… कितने हफ़्ते चली?

- **Facts:**
  - *Kismet* (1943), directed by Gyan Mukherjee, starring Ashok Kumar. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kismet_(1943_film))
  - Bombay: premiered 9 January 1943 at Roxy Talkies, with a run of over 50 weeks. (same page)
  - Calcutta: opened 24 September 1943 at Roxy Cinema and ran 187 consecutive weeks. (same page)
  - Plot: a pickpocket turns out to be the long-lost son of the man he robbed. (same page)
- **Payoff:** 187 weeks in one Calcutta cinema: more than three and a half years.
- **Images:** [`File:Kismet-1943-movie-poster.jpg`](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Kismet-1943-movie-poster.jpg) is PD-India, with no US tag, so record it as PD-India only. Check the other files in `Category:Kismet (1943 film)` one by one.
- **Lock before use:** The article has an "AI-generated text" maintenance banner. The 187-week and 50-week figures need a book or film-heritage source before the payoff is written.

### 7. `pyaasa` — Guru Dutt को Pyaasa की ending बदलनी पड़ी?

  - It became one of the most commercially successful films of 1957. (same page)
- **Payoff:** Guru Dutt wanted Vijay "left all alone"; distributors wanted a change, and writer Abrar Alvi argued for a compromise.
- **Images:** V&A poster [O68657](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O68657/pyaasa-1957-eternal-thirst-indian-film-poster/): check the licence/credit on the page.
- **Lock before use:** Check the *Time* claim against Time's own list. Keep the Sahir link "surmised". Leave out the ₹29 million figure unless it's sourced.

## How to produce the next one

Copy the template, then:

```powershell
# from repo root
npm run short -- lint SLUG
npm run short -- say SLUG
npm run short -- still SLUG --url IMAGE --source PAGE --credit "Studio" --label "Film (Year): what it shows" --name f1
npm run short -- stills SLUG
npm run short -- render SLUG --draft
# watch draft.mp4; then voice (record or --edge) and full render
```

Checklist before render: hook on frame 0, source on every fact, no textbook Hindi, stills labelled, captions above the Shorts UI, loop shares a hook word.
